15,000 rooms, one answer

Finding a suitable room took anything from half an hour to weeks, and the institution’s own records disagreed about what its rooms held.

Who

Client
A large UK university
Scale
More than 15,000 rooms in over 40 buildings
My role
Designed and built it
Status
Working prototype; pilot proposed

What

To find a room that fitted a class, staff asked colleagues (about half an hour), searched booking systems (about 45 minutes), walked the campus (about two hours), or raised a ticket (hours to weeks). The records behind those systems did not agree with each other: a room’s furniture count and its timetabled seats could differ.

How

  1. Used what already existed. Floor plans, furniture layouts, teaching profiles and occupancy surveys, joined into one place, with nothing new to collect.
  2. Made one search answer the real question: which room, where, how many seats, set up for what kind of teaching.
  3. Listed every contradiction instead of hiding it. Where two records disagree, the system does not pick a winner; it lists the conflict so the owner can fix it at source.
  4. Proposed how to prove it: a six-week pilot, measured on how long it takes to answer real questions.

Impact

  • Contradictions visibleconflicts between records listed for correction at source
