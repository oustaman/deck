A failing programme back on track, and 15–20 hours a week down to two

A group of companies had no shared way to file or find its contracts, patents and agreements. The programme meant to fix it was failing.

Client
The board of a group of five companies
Region
Macau, Taiwan, Guangzhou and Hong Kong: four legal systems
People
About 50, of whom about 40 in mainland China and Macau
My role
Brought in to rescue the programme

The situation

Each company kept its documents its own way. Finding one agreement for an audit meant asking around, calling and meeting. Companies in the group sometimes worked on the same thing without knowing. The programme to fix this had stalled: designers wanted freedom, engineers wanted structure, management wanted savings, and nobody held them together.

What I did

  1. Started with the board, not the backlog. We went through the brief line by line, cut what they did not need, and agreed what could actually be delivered.
  2. Gave each team its own brief inside the shared plan, with short check-ins every three days, under strict confidentiality.
  3. Made one rule for naming every document in every company, so a file’s name alone says whose it is, what kind it is and when it was made.
  4. Added one field everyone could see: active projects. Each company could now see what the others were working on.

What changed

  • 15–20 hours → about 2a week across the group, searching and meeting about documents
  • Duplicated work stoppedthe active-projects field showed who was already doing what
  • Chat channels closedthe ones that existed only to find documents
  • Adopted immediatelythe one objection came from the person whose manual filing the system replaced
